WHEEL · WALL · WORLD

Daytime is a personal operating system for the parts of a life that don't fit in one list — work, health, money, the people you're coaching with, the routine that keeps the rest of it running. It holds one set of facts and shows it to you three different ways, depending on what you're actually trying to do in the moment.

SYS.01

Wheel

what needs you today

The command view. A spoke for every domain of your life — Work, Self-Care, Finances, Sessions, Daily Routine — each carrying its own tasks, goals, and reference material.

The ring around each spoke reads two things at a glance: how much that domain is carrying (how far the arc reaches — longer means more still open), and how hot the hottest of it is (its color — blue is low priority, amber is medium, red is high). A spoke you've cleared draws no arc at all. The center number is everything across every spoke that has a claim on today, in the color of the most urgent thing in it.

Some spokes — Daily Routine, 60 Second Reset — aren't task lists at all. They open straight to the procedure itself, because that spoke is the document.

Wall

Résumé — current draft

The reference library. Notes, source documents, links, the exact things you'd otherwise print out and pin up — filed under a spoke, searchable, sorted by what you've pinned and what you've touched most recently.

Anything pulled from a real document stays word for word. Daytime doesn't summarize or improve source material — if it's on the Wall, it's the actual text, so you can trust it the way you'd trust the original.

SYS.03

World

the day itself

The calendar — but it isn't only events. Pick a day and it shows one merged schedule: calendar blocks and any Wheel task with a time on it, in clock order. Anything without a time — a someday task, an all-day block — sits underneath as also today.

A quiet dot on the month grid also marks any day something new was pinned to the Wall — so World doubles as a record of when reference material actually entered the system, not just what's scheduled.

Put it on your phone

iPhone · Safari

It runs full screen with no browser bar, like any other app — and it's the same workspace as everyone else signed in.

  1. 1 Open the Daytime link in Safari. This has to be Safari — it doesn't work from Chrome or another browser on iPhone.
  2. 2 Tap the Share icon at the bottom of the screen — the square with an arrow pointing up.
  3. 3 Scroll down the list and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. 4 Rename it if you want, then tap Add in the top right.

Put it on your computer

Windows · Chrome or Edge

Same idea as the phone: it gets its own window and its own taskbar icon, with no address bar and no tabs. Chrome and Edge both do this; Firefox does not.

  1. 1 Open the Daytime link in Chrome or Edge and sign in first, so the installed app opens straight into your workspace.
  2. 2 Look at the right-hand end of the address bar for the install icon — a small monitor with an arrow pointing down into it. Click it, then click Install.
  3. 3 No icon there? Use the menu instead. In Chrome: ⋮ → Cast, save and share → Install page as app. In Edge: ⋯ → Apps → Install this site as an app.
  4. 4 Daytime opens in its own window. Right-click its icon in the taskbar and choose Pin to taskbar so it stays there after you close it. Edge offers a “Pin to taskbar” tickbox right after installing — same thing.
On a Mac, it's the same first three steps in Chrome or Edge. The app lands in your Applications folder; to keep it in the Dock, open it, then right-click its Dock icon and choose Options → Keep in Dock.

First five minutes

  1. Open Wheel, tap a spoke, add today's first task. Give it a priority — that's what colors its arc.
  2. Pin a real document to the Wall, under that same spoke. Paste it as written — Daytime keeps it that way.
  3. Give the task a date and time. Watch it appear on World without entering it twice.
  4. Check World each morning; read the ring on the Wheel. One tells you what's happening, the other what's hot.
