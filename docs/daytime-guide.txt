DAYTIME — HOW IT WORKS

Daytime is a personal operating system for the parts of a life that don't fit
in one list — work, health, money, the people you're coaching with, the
routine that keeps the rest of it running. It holds one set of facts and
shows it to you three different ways, depending on what you're actually
trying to do in the moment: Wheel, Wall, World.


WHEEL — what needs you today
-----------------------------
The command view. A spoke for every domain of your life — Work, Self-Care,
Finances, Sessions, Daily Routine, Personal — each carrying its own tasks,
goals, and reference material.

The ring around each spoke reads two things at a glance: how much that
domain is carrying (how far the arc reaches — longer means more still
open), and how hot the hottest of it is (its color — blue is low priority,
amber is medium, red is high). A spoke you've cleared draws no arc at all.
The center number is everything across every spoke that has a claim on
today, in the color of the most urgent thing in it.

Some spokes — Daily Routine, 60 Second Reset — aren't task lists at all.
They open straight to the procedure itself, because that spoke IS the
document.


WALL — what you'd pin above a desk
------------------------------------
The reference library. Notes, source documents, links — the exact things
you'd otherwise print out and pin up — filed under a spoke, searchable,
sorted by what you've pinned and what you've touched most recently.

Anything pulled from a real document stays word for word. Daytime doesn't
summarize or improve source material — if it's on the Wall, it's the actual
text, so you can trust it the way you'd trust the original.


WORLD — the day itself
------------------------
The calendar — but it isn't only events. Pick a day and it shows one merged
schedule: calendar blocks and any Wheel task with a time on it, in clock
order. Anything without a time — a someday task, an all-day block — sits
underneath as "also today."

A quiet dot on the month grid also marks any day something new was pinned
to the Wall, so World doubles as a record of when reference material
actually entered the system, not just what's scheduled.


ONE DATASET, THREE LENSES
---------------------------
Nothing is re-entered. Give a Wheel task a date and it's already on the
calendar. Link it to a Wall document and that document's chip follows the
task everywhere it appears. Tap any of it and the item opens in place — you
never actually leave the view you were in.


WORKING FROM TWO PLACES
-------------------------
Sign in and the same workspace follows you to every device, and to anyone
else who's been invited. Changes sync in under a second, and a small bell
shows what the other person added — with a name and a timestamp, not just
a day.


SIGNING IN
------------
Tap "Continue with Google" and pick your account — that's the whole thing,
no password, no code to type.

IMPORTANT: you have to sign in with the specific Gmail address that was
invited to this workspace — not just any Google account. The app is
running in a testing mode with Google, so only addresses that have been
added by name will be let in. If Google says something like "this app is
currently being tested," it means you're signed into the wrong Google
account on this device — switch accounts and try again with the right one.


PUTTING IT ON YOUR PHONE (iPhone / Safari)
---------------------------------------------
1. Open the Daytime link in Safari (it has to be Safari — this doesn't
   work from Chrome or another browser on iPhone).
2. Tap the Share icon at the bottom of the screen — the square with an
   arrow pointing up.
3. Scroll down the list of options and tap "Add to Home Screen."
4. Rename it if you want, then tap "Add" in the top right.

It now sits on your home screen like any other app — full screen, no
browser bar, and it's the same workspace as everyone else signed in.


PUTTING IT ON YOUR COMPUTER (Windows / Chrome or Edge)
---------------------------------------------------------
Same idea as the phone: Daytime gets its own window and its own taskbar
icon, with no address bar and no tabs. Chrome and Edge both do this;
Firefox does not.

1. Open the Daytime link in Chrome or Edge and sign in first, so the
   installed app opens straight into your workspace.
2. Look at the right-hand end of the address bar for the install icon — a
   small monitor with an arrow pointing down into it. Click it, then click
   "Install."
3. No icon there? Use the menu instead. In Chrome: ⋮ → Cast, save and share
   → Install page as app. In Edge: ⋯ → Apps → Install this site as an app.
4. Daytime opens in its own window. Right-click its icon in the taskbar and
   choose "Pin to taskbar" so it stays there after you close it. Edge offers
   a "Pin to taskbar" tickbox right after installing — same thing.

On a Mac it's the same first three steps in Chrome or Edge. The app lands in
your Applications folder; to keep it in the Dock, open it, then right-click
its Dock icon and choose Options → Keep in Dock.


FIRST FIVE MINUTES
---------------------
1. Open Wheel, tap a spoke, add today's first task. Give it a priority —
   that's what colors its arc.
2. Pin a real document to the Wall, under that same spoke. Paste it as
   written — Daytime keeps it that way.
3. Give the task a date and time. Watch it appear on World without
   entering it twice.
4. Check World each morning; read the ring on the Wheel. One tells you
   what's happening, the other what's hot.
